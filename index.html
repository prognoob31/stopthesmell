Why nothing you’ve tried has worked

Because “the smell” isn’t one problem. It’s several, and each one needs a different fix. Wash a bacterial infection with soap and you feed it. Ignore a forgotten tampon and it gets worse every day. Here’s what different smells often mean:

  • A fishy smell, especially after sex or on your period, with thin grey or white discharge

    Often: bacterial vaginosis (BV), the most common vaginal problem in women aged 15 to 44. The good bacteria drop and the smell-causing ones take over.

    What to do: Soap won’t fix it. BV is treated with antibiotics from a doctor. The guide shows you how to spot it and what to ask for.

  • It got worse after you started using an intimate wash, Dettol, salt water, toilet soap or herbal mixes

    Often: washing inside, or using harsh products outside, upsets the bacteria and acidity that keep the vagina healthy. Douching is linked to more BV.

    What to do: Stop putting things inside. Chapter 2 shows what to do instead, and it’s simpler and cheaper.

  • A sweaty, musky smell that builds up through the day

    Often: sweat, damp or synthetic underwear, and moisture trapped in the folds.

    What to do: Underwear, drying and washing habits. Small changes, covered in Chapter 2.

  • A stronger, sour or metallic smell on your period

    Often: blood meeting air and bacteria, made worse by pads or tampons kept on too long.

    What to do: Change your timing, your products and what you clean with. Chapter 4.

  • A smell right after sex

    Often: semen is alkaline and temporarily shifts the vagina’s acidity. That usually settles. If it lingers, BV may be involved.

    What to do: Know what’s normal and what isn’t, and how to talk to your partner about it. Chapter 4.

  • A strong smell with frothy yellow or green discharge

    Often: trichomoniasis, a sexually transmitted infection that can smell fishy or musty.

    What to do: See a doctor and get tested. Your partner may need treatment too. The guide shows you what to ask and how to bring it up.

  • A rotten smell that came out of nowhere

    Often: a forgotten tampon or a condom left inside. It can smell like rotten meat.

    What to do: See a doctor today. Don’t try to wash it away.

Different causes, different fixes. Guess wrong and you waste money and make it worse.

This guide tells you which one you’re dealing with, what to change, and when to stop and see a doctor. A mild, natural scent is normal. A strong, fishy, foul or new smell is your body telling you something, and you’re about to find out what.
